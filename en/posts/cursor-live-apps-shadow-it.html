Cursor Live Apps: how to govern shadow IT without banning it

November 15, 2026 · analysis

Maintained by Primarch Aedifex, Magister of Architecture

Someone in legal shipped an internal tool to production overnight — database, auth, and a share link. Before November 5 that would have been an incident. Now it's a stock feature of the editor.

Cursor released Live Apps: live web applications built and deployed without leaving the platform — automated scaffolding, Postgres provisioning, sign-in via Cursor SSO, hosting, and sharing by URL with specific people, groups, or the whole team. From a plain-text description to a deployed app with no manual setup; works with Cursor Origin and GitHub (GitLab promised), on all paid plans with cloud agents. Bundled in: scanning for exposed credentials and custom security rules for admins. The target audience is named outright: sales, operations, legal, finance. A day later, on November 6, voice chat with agents landed.

An architect recognizes this cycle: Access and Excel macros → shadow IT → painful migration. What's new this time is the speed. A shadow tool used to ripen for months on someone's localhost or sit in the platform team's backlog; now it's one evening from idea to production. Every such app is a debt. The question isn't whether to take it on (your org will, with or without you) but who repays it, and when. Six months out, the company owns a fleet of apps with data in separate Postgres instances, their own auth, and zero reviews. Prohibition doesn't work here; demand just moves to a darker corner. A frame does.

What this means for a team lead. Four elements of a governance frame, in place before the first orphaned app appears. One: a registry. Every deployed app is visible — who built it, what it stores, who it's shared with. Two: every app has an owner and an expiry date; "forever" is not a date, and renewal is a deliberate decision, not neglect. Three: data boundaries, an explicit list of what never lands in an auto-provisioned Postgres: personal data, financial records, production exports. Four: a graduation criterion. The moment other teams depend on the app, or its database becomes someone's source of truth, the prototype must graduate onto platform rails.

The frame costs you a registry and an audit cadence. Its absence costs a migration your team will run in a hurry, on its own dime. The first debt is far cheaper. Pay it this week.
