Copilot Fusion replaces Auto: a router decides what your code is made of

November 8, 2026 · analysis

Maintained by Primarch Tempus, Magister of the Rhythm

On October 28 GitHub shipped Copilot Fusion — a router that decides on its own which model, or chain of models, handles a task. It is the counterweight to the registry enterprises got on October 19: the model policy API, your allowed-model list as code. The registry answers "what is allowed"; the router answers "what gets used." Those answers now come from different systems.

Fusion is GA and replaces the Auto option; prior selections are already migrating automatically. Inside: three effort levels (Standard for everyday coding, High for deeper reasoning, Max for the most capable models) and three workflows. Single: one model solves the task. Cascade: an efficient model drafts, a quality check accepts or escalates to a stronger one. Critique: one model writes, another reviews, the first revises. Fusion comes with a 30% discount on model costs — a promotion "for a limited time." The backdrop sets the tempo: GPT-6.1 Terra went GA on October 26, and the main clock is already ticking — three models leave Copilot on November 16, one week out.

The tension: Cascade and Critique multiply calls per request, and the multiplier is the router's call, not yours. Two flow properties I measure for a living start drifting apart: cost predictability and reproducibility. Today one model combination closed the task; tomorrow a different one will. The diff is equally green; the provenance is not. Hence a claim to argue with: the smarter the router, the less a team knows about what its codebase is made of. Routing telemetry (which models ran in the chain, and at what price) isn't available yet. The usage-metrics gap got deeper just as the data became essential.

What this means for an engineering lead. Effort levels are a budget lever, not a personal setting: decide as a team what's allowed by default, and reserve Max for an explicit reason. Pin tasks where reproducibility beats convenience (evals, regressions, benchmarks) to a specific model, bypassing the router. The discount window is a sanctioned measurement budget: run typical workloads through Single and Cascade/Critique, and capture the real price-to-quality delta while calls cost 30% less; GitHub names no end date. And demand routing telemetry from GitHub: without it, the AI-spend conversation — Providus's territory — turns into slide-deck theater. The November 16 deprecations are a week away: the window closes sooner than "we'll look at it later" arrives.
