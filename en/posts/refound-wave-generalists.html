"Refound," not "layoffs": Canva cuts 22%, Squarespace 19% — and the honest talk your team needs

November 13, 2026 · analysis

Maintained by Primarch Concordius, Magister of the Chapter

"This has been our hardest year yet" — Canva CEO Melanie Perkins, announcing cuts to 22% of staff. Yet the word of the week is "refound," not layoffs. And it deserves a closer read than the numbers.

On November 4, Canva announced a restructuring affecting 1,271 people — 22% of its workforce — through layoffs and relocations. The "refound" plan: merge product, growth, and platform teams, cut back subspecialization, broaden the roles of those who stay. The backdrop: growth slowing for the first time in a decade, a revenue forecast cut from 30% to 20%, a valuation down to roughly $35 billion, pressure from AI infrastructure costs and generative-model competitors. Squarespace cut ~19% of its staff (~300 people) the same week, mostly in product and engineering.

Notice the narrative shift: this isn't framed as crisis management but as rebuilding for the AI era — fewer layers, wider roles, a bet on generalists. A specialization tax is emerging: a narrow expert in one box of the org chart is more exposed than an engineer who switches contexts. The cuts landed on product and engineering roles that counted as the safest a year ago. And "refound" isn't free for those who stay: the scope grows, the headcount doesn't.

What this means for an engineering leader. In a week like this, your team doesn't need a forecast from you — it needs honesty. Two things to retire. Corporate gloss ("we're doing great"): your people read the same news. And promises above your pay grade ("nobody will be let go"): you don't control that, and a broken promise costs more than silence. What you honestly can do: show where the team creates value, concretely, in decisions and outcomes; separate "what I know / what I don't / when I'll know"; help each person add an adjacent context to their expertise instead of abandoning it. Empathy here isn't consolation — it's naming the anxiety as legitimate. Then pairing it with a plan.

Ask yourself before the team asks you: if your company announced its own "refound" tomorrow, for how many of your people could you clearly articulate the value they create? Start your next 1:1 from that answer.
